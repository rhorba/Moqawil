### 2026-08-13 SCOPE — Nominee/prête-nom structure considered and rejected; AE+CNDP path parked pending employment change (informational discussion — no code/infra changed)
- **Specialist**: Orchestrator, on direct user instruction (advisory conversation, continuation of 2026-08-12 business-structure thread)
- **Summary**: Owner explored a "first move" of selling Moqawil (private repo, closed-source) to individual AE clients who self-host, paid ~$500 USD or less via bank transfer or cash. Advised: register as AE before taking payment regardless of payment method (cash vs. transfer doesn't change the underlying tax/registration obligation, and cash has no protective effect — it just removes the paper trail); confirmed CNDP does **not** apply to this specific "buyer self-hosts" model since each buyer is their own data controller (distinct from the managed-cloud-tier scenario in the 2026-08-12 entry, where the operator hosts others' data and CNDP does apply). Owner then proposed routing the business through a friend who is already AE — friend invoices and receives payment, owner stays off the paperwork, they split the proceeds — revealing the real driver: owner is currently employed in public administration (fonctionnaire) and cannot personally register as AE / run a private commercial activity without authorization under the Statut Général de la Fonction Publique's "non-cumul d'activités" principle.
- **Assessment**: rejected as high-risk, not a viable workaround. Issues flagged: (1) using a friend's AE identity to invoice for a business he doesn't actually operate is "facturation de complaisance" (accommodation invoicing) — real fraud exposure for both parties, not an administrative technicality; (2) friend would carry legal/tax liability (his own 200K/yr and 80K/client caps, CNSS/AMO, activity-type declarations) for turnover and data he doesn't control; (3) direct self-contradiction in the plan — owner intended to personally operate the hosting and file the CNDP declaration (making owner the real *responsable de traitement*) while the commercial/invoicing identity would be the friend's, a controller-identity mismatch that itself draws scrutiny; (4) the core purpose was concealing outside commercial activity from a government employer, which is a disciplinary matter (up to termination) independent of the tax issues. Declined to help design the concealment mechanics (split, invoicing flow, or anything to keep owner's name off paperwork while retaining control).
- **Decision**: owner parked the friend-front plan. Confirmed instead: **if/when owner moves from public administration to private-sector employment**, the civil-service non-cumul restriction no longer applies (private employment is governed by the Code du Travail + individual contract, which has no equivalent blanket ban) — owner could then register as AE and run the managed-hosting business + file the CNDP declaration under their own identity, per the original 2026-08-12 phased plan, with no nominee needed. Caveat flagged for that future point: check the new employment contract for exclusivity/non-compete clauses and general duty-of-loyalty limits, especially if the new employer is in a competing field (compliance/SaaS/fintech).
- **Follow-up / next session**: owner wants to run a **preprod deployment test using CI/CD**, using **cheaper hosting options** (boutique EU VPS — Hetzner/Scaleway/OVHcloud — rather than a hyperscaler) for the test, consistent with the hosting-cost comparison already logged 2026-08-12. Not yet scoped as a sprint task.
- **Status**: parked — no AE registration, no CNDP filing, no friend-front structure actioned. Revisit AE/CNDP path only if/when owner's employment situation changes. Preprod CI/CD test is a pending next-session item, unscoped.
- **Impact**: high — determines whether/when the owner can personally operate Moqawil's paid tiers at all; v0.1 (self-host, AGPL, no owner-run hosting) is unaffected either way.

### 2026-08-12 SCOPE — Owner's business-structure + hosting-provider plan for the managed cloud tier (not v0.1, informational discussion — no code/infra changed)
- **Specialist**: Orchestrator, on direct user instruction (advisory conversation, logged for later reference)
- **Summary**: Owner asked how Moqawil's future managed cloud tier (CLAUDE.md §1 "Optional managed cloud tier post-launch") should be structured legally and where to host it. Discussed and the owner settled on a phased plan:
  - **Legal entity**: start as Auto-Entrepreneur (the owner themself, using Moqawil's own regime) rather than a US LLC. A foreign-owned US LLC was considered and rejected as the *starting* structure — it adds Office des Changes exposure (Moroccan-resident ownership of a foreign entity needs declaration/authorization), mandatory IRS Form 5472 filing even at zero activity (min penalty $25k for a miss), and does not remove Moroccan worldwide-income tax residency, so it would add a second compliance surface without removing the first. Plan is to scale later to a **SARL Maroc** (direct fit with Moroccan law, eligible for services-export IS incentives) or a **hybrid** (SARL as the operating entity + a US LLC/Stripe Atlas used only as a payment pass-through for USD/EUR billing, funds repatriated to the SARL as service-export revenue) once AE's 200,000 MAD/year or 80,000 MAD/client caps are close to binding.
  - **CNDP**: hosting on AWS's EU region (or an EU-based alternative) is a cross-border personal-data transfer under Loi 09-08, which needs a **déclaration normale** (processing) *and* a separate **demande d'autorisation** (transfer abroad — a real CNDP-council approval, not a fast notification, can take weeks-to-months) — not just one filing. This obligation attaches to the owner as *responsable de traitement* only once the **managed cloud tier actually launches and starts processing real customer data**; self-hosters remain their own responsable de traitement and are unaffected. Not a v0.1 blocker (v0.1 is self-host only per the Definition of Done) — the CNDP filing should start ~1-2 months before the managed tier's public launch, not on launch day, given the authorization step's timeline. Retention duration to declare: 10 years, matching CGI Article 211's existing invoice-retention rule.
  - **Hosting cost**: for this workload (single Docker Compose monolith — Next.js + Postgres + Caddy), a hyperscaler (AWS/GCP/Azure) is not actually the cheapest fit. Of the three, GCP was assessed as cheapest for sustained small-VM workloads (automatic sustained-use discounts, real always-free e2-micro tier); AWS's egress pricing is the most expensive of the three. But boutique EU VPS providers (Hetzner, Scaleway, OVHcloud) were flagged as ~10x cheaper than any hyperscaler for this exact workload while still qualifying as EU-based for CNDP transfer-adequacy purposes, and align with CLAUDE.md §4 Feature 6's "no paid third-party services required" self-host ethos. No hosting decision was finalized in this conversation — flagged for a real System Designer pass (Framework Rule 1) when the managed-cloud-tier sprint is actually scoped.
- **Status**: informational / not yet actioned — no sprint scoped, no infra provisioned, no CNDP filing started. Revisit with System Designer + Software Architect (Framework Rule 1) before the managed cloud tier sprint begins.
- **Impact**: medium — shapes a future sprint's legal/infra scope, but v0.1 (self-host only) is unaffected.
